Research note

Festivals as demand shocks on the Indian grid.

How much does Diwali actually move electricity demand — and can telling the model help it forecast the day ahead? A decade of weather-adjusted, per-festival estimates on Delhi's peak demand, a data-integrity audit that had hidden the answer, and the disciplined way the signed effect now rides in production.

Rupesh Bharambe · Gridalytics · daily peak demand, 2015–2024

Quantification · the main result

A decade of per-festival effects

An event-study regression on ten years of Delhi's daily peak demand (PSP, 2015–2024). Each effect is the festival day's deviation from a weather-adjusted counterfactual — temperature, humidity, rain, day-of-week and year-month fixed effects all removed first — so what's left is the festival, not the season. The sign varies: most festivals suppress demand (offices and industry shut), a few raise it. A single binary is_festival flag can express none of this.

Effect on daily peak vs counterfactual, with 95% CI. N = 3,569 days, HAC(7) errors; the top seven survive placebo-randomization inference by >3 SD. Per-year stable — Holi lands between −27% and −34% in every year 2015–2024.

Festivals aren't one day. Diwali is a three-day trough — Diwali −17%, Govardhan Puja (+1) −19%, Bhai Duj (+2) −12% — and Holi carries a −13% day-after. A one-day flag, even a per-festival identity, can't carry that shape; a signed magnitude can.

Method

A causal, leak-free effect — identical at train and serve

The magnitude the model consumes is expected_event_effect_pct, built in src/forecasting/event_effects.py. It never sees its own outcome or any future year's, so it is honest inside walk-forward CV and recomputed the same way at inference.

Why this beats a flag. With roughly one occurrence per year, a gradient-boosted tree can't isolate a rare event from a binary dummy — it has nowhere near enough splits. A signed, pre-computed magnitude hands the model the answer as a continuous prior instead of asking it to rediscover a once-a-year pattern. It's a rare-event encoding, not a new estimator.
Data integrity · why the answer was hidden

Three silent defects manufactured a false negative

An earlier pass concluded festivals barely mattered and a magnitude feature was neutral. That conclusion was an artifact: the data was quietly broken, each defect nudging the result toward "no effect." Fixing them is what turned a null into the table above — the project's honest-numbers rule in action.

Forecasting value · the study

The signed magnitude cuts festival-day error

Rolling-origin backtest on Delhi, LightGBM forecasting log peak, test years 2019–2024, 146 festival test days. The counterfactual for the magnitude feature is fit only on data before each test year — fully causal. On festival days the signed magnitude is the clear winner; on normal days (where the feature is exactly 0) everything barely moves, which is how you know the gain is the event signal and not global perturbation.

In production · the disciplined part

Shipped only where it earns its place

The same signed-magnitude idea is wired into the state fleet as expected_event_effect_pct, estimated causally from each state's own peak series. Before shipping it anywhere, it was ablated across 12 states — and kept only where it beat that state's own baseline.

Across the 12-state ablation the feature helps Punjab clearly (+0.18 pts) and Tamil Nadu marginally (+0.05, within fold noise). Everywhere else it is flat or slightly negative — it actively hurts Odisha's short, drift-heavy series.

So it ships to Punjab alone. On Delhi, where rich lag features already encode most of the one-step signal, the magnitude isn't in the champion either. The quantification is real and large; the forecasting lever is narrow — and the model only carries it where the back-test says it pays.

Honest limitations & what's next

Where this stops, and where it goes

  • Daily peak, one city. The decade-long study is Delhi's peak demand only; the production feature generalizes per state, but the causal estimates are only as deep as each state's history.
  • Placebo caveat. HAC(7) errors are anti-conservative here (22% of placebo shifts reach p<0.05); the paper draft moves to randomization/block-bootstrap inference. The top seven effects survive regardless.
  • Intraday shape (open). Festivals reshape the evening peak, which no daily scalar can express. That needs a 5-minute backfill around event dates — the one lever with real upside left.
  • Calendar freshness (open). Lunar festival dates shift yearly and the hardcoded calendar stops at 2026; a schema-validated LLM pass would keep it current and catch ad-hoc events (a cricket final, a heatwave advisory).
